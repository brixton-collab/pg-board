First, a clip has to have failed in one of your three ways — they left at the hook (average watch under 6.5 seconds on a clip that reached 3,000+), it got reach but nobody followed (8,000+ reach under 3 follows per 1,000), or it converted but nobody saw it (over 3 per 1,000 on under 6,000 reach). That 7.8 is the only benchmark on this page, and it is the best rate you have ever managed on a crowd over 50,000 — your own number, never anyone else's. It deliberately is NOT your best rate ever (24.2 per 1,000, off a clip that reached 19,215); rate falls as reach grows, so applying a small clip's rate to 1.96M people invents followers that were never available. The ceiling line on each card — how big the format could go — reads off the same 7.8, which is why the two numbers now agree instead of sitting 3x apart.

Under 250 followers it is small ball and it comes off the board — with one exception: if the reach is still arriving, it stays, because those followers expire whether or not the idea is big. A format needs 3+ clips before it gets a ceiling at all; one clip is an anecdote, and the board should not send you to re-shoot an idea on the strength of the clip that just failed.

Eight things to shoot.
Read the card, go film it.

Every card is a hook you read word for word, three bullets on how to shoot it, and one line each on what makes it convert and what makes it travel — so you know what the shot is for while you're standing there. Nothing else. Four are golf, four aren't, which is John's rule, not mine.

The four that apply to every card
  • Frame one is never your face. It's the object, the number, or the other person.
  • Roll before you know the answer. If you already know how it ends, your face knows too.
  • Report it, never prescribe it. Last line is what you learned, past tense — never "you should."
  • One phone, no operator. Back camera for the thing, then flip to the front camera for the reaction.
